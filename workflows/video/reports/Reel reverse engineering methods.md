# Measure first, then fit by rendering

The best 2025-2026 approach is not a single model. It is a CPU pipeline of classical measurement plus small learned components, with our own renderer acting as the final judge. No published tool turns a motion-graphics video into editable keyframes. Benchmarks keep finding that vision-language models (VLMs) copy how a shot looks far better than when and how it moves. So the core of the system should be: track each element, fit keyframes and eases to the tracked curves, then render and compare to correct what is left. On a 4-core, no-GPU container this is realistic for everything except three jobs: pixel-accurate prop masks, RGBA layer separation, and true 3D camera solves. Those are the only reasons to rent a GPU. Concretely, adopt TransNetV2 plus hand-written signal detectors for cuts; OpenCV DIS flow with RANSAC similarity fits for the 2.5D camera; RapidOCR word boxes and a DINOv2 font shortlist checked by rendering candidate fonts; ruptures segmentation plus least-squares cubic-Bézier fitting for eases; faster-whisper, htdemucs and beat_this for audio; and CMA-ES only for small residual corrections. The one advantage nobody else has is that our template engine can render unlimited exactly-labelled training clips. That makes a synthetic benchmark, and retraining on it, the centre of the self-improvement loop. Caveat: none of the runtimes below were measured in this container, and many paper details were read from search snippets because arxiv.org and huggingface.co were blocked. Treat every number as a lead to benchmark locally first.

## VLMs reproduce appearance but not timing, so the core must be tracking plus fitting

The closest prior art all splits the problem into a layer representation plus fitted per-frame transforms. **Fast Sprite Decomposition** (CyberAgent, ECCV 2024, MIT code) models each sprite as a static texture plus per-frame affine matrices and opacity, fitted to raster video by gradient optimisation. It ships the **Crello Animation** dataset ([GitHub](https://github.com/CyberAgentAILab/sprite-decompose); [paper](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/papers/08402.pdf)). **Motion Vectorization** (SIGGRAPH Asia 2023) converts motion-graphics video into an editable SVG motion program that can be retimed to music beats ([arXiv 2309.14642](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.14642)). The sprite representation is in effect an After Effects precomp with baked transforms, so it maps directly onto our template parameters.

The end-to-end alternatives are not ready for our use. **OmniLottie** (CVPR 2026) includes a video-to-Lottie task, but it is a 4B model needing **about 15.2 GB of GPU memory and 8-133 s per generation**. Its code is Apache-2.0, but its MMLottie-2M training data is **CC BY-NC-SA** ([GitHub](https://github.com/OpenVGLab/OmniLottie); [dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/OmniLottie/MMLottie-2M)). **Animation2Code** (NeurIPS 2026, 1,069 web-animation videos) finds that VLMs reach up to **0.84 appearance similarity, but temporal fidelity is the weak point** ([project](https://anya-ji.github.io/animation2code-website); [arXiv 2606.28593](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28593)). Commercial "video to Lottie" converters only wrap raster frames in timing JSON ([EasyLottie](https://www.easylottie.com/en/blog/videos-to-lottie)). Timing and easing are what the user notices most, so they cannot be delegated to a language model. VLMs earn a place only as labellers: naming a layer, or breaking ties between archetypes.

## Stage by stage on four CPU cores

### Cuts need learned candidates plus signal detectors tuned to our transition kit

**TransNetV2** is MIT-licensed, ships PyTorch and TensorFlow inference, and scores **F1 96.2 on BBC Planet Earth and 77.9 on ClipShots** ([GitHub](https://github.com/soCzech/TransNetV2)). It has a separate all-frames head for gradual transitions, which handles them better than PySceneDetect ([CineTrans, arXiv 2508.11484](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.11484)). Its weakness matters to us because we measure cut timing to the frame. A 2026 study finds TransNetV2 and AutoShot "usually choose the start frame in the middle of the dissolve", and AutoShot reaches only **0.252 Transition IoU** ([OmniShotCut, arXiv 2604.24762](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24762v1)). On short-video data, gradual transitions caused **54% of AutoShot's misses**, and PySceneDetect scored **F1 < 0.6** ([AutoShot, CVPRW 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023W/NAS/papers/Zhu_AutoShot_A_Short_Video_Dataset_and_State-of-the-Art_Shot_Boundary_Detection_CVPRW_2023_paper.pdf)).

So treat model output as candidate windows only. Refine and classify each window with per-frame curves, one per transition in the kit: a mean-luma spike means a flash; a dip in Laplacian variance means lens blur; one-directional DIS-flow magnitude with lost high frequencies means a whip pan; offset colour channels mean an RGB-split glitch; a moving boundary in per-row or per-column difference maps means a wipe; and a low-residual global similarity fit means a push or slide. This is our design, not a published method. Newer systems that classify transition types, such as OmniShotCut and TransVLM ([project](https://chence17.github.io/TransVLM/)), are worth watching, but their code and licences could not be checked. No public benchmark covers motion-graphics transitions, so our own rendered transitions become the test set.

### Archetype matching must combine an image embedding with motion descriptors

With about 40 archetypes, the deciding factor is motion, and frame embeddings miss it. On **CameraBench** (NeurIPS 2025), generative VLMs beat embedding baselines for camera-motion retrieval ([paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/c2d7b739f61ffbea729dad2cf9ec8c60-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf)). **SigLIP 2** is strong for zero-shot image classification and retrieval but is evaluated on images ([arXiv 2502.14786](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.14786)). The recommended matcher builds a vector from SigLIP 2 embeddings of 3-5 keyframes (layout: paper card, phone mock-up, headline), the measured camera curve (total zoom, pan direction, roll, ease class, parallax layer count), shot duration, in and out transition types, and the OCR text-area fraction. Classify it with k-NN or logistic regression, validated leave-one-reel-out on r1-r6. Otherwise "push-in on headline" and "pan across headline" look the same. **Qwen3-VL-2B** has official GGUF builds and llama.cpp support from October 2025 ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct-GGUF)). Use it for offline library labelling with forced multiple-choice JSON, not as the source of motion facts.

### The 2.5D camera is a 2D problem per layer, not a 3D reconstruction

After Effects 2.5D scenes are flat cards seen through a perspective camera, so feed-forward 3D solvers model the wrong world. They also cost too much: VGGT takes **0.2 s on an H100** and needs a GPU throughout ([CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Wang_VGGT_Visual_Geometry_Grounded_Transformer_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf)). Learned point trackers are benchmarked only on GPUs. On an H100, CoTracker3 runs at 102 FPS and TAPNext at 189 FPS with 256 points ([TAPNext++, arXiv 2604.10582](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10582v1)). The approach that matches how AE scenes are built is:

1. Compute OpenCV DIS flow at about 540x960.
2. Fit a RANSAC similarity per frame and decompose it into pan, zoom and roll. Use a homography only when a card visibly tilts in Y.
3. Cluster the leftover flow into layers. Each layer's ratio of translation to camera translation approximates 1/z, which gives the z-order for a rebuilt fake camera.

Mask out screen-locked overlays first (grain, paper, film edge) and skip transition windows, or flow will be biased toward zero. NeuFlow v2 (Apache-2.0) runs at over 20 FPS on a Jetson Orin Nano and is the learned-flow fallback for low-texture shots ([arXiv 2408.10161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10161)). Its CPU speed is unknown.

### Elements and masks should use cheap graphic cues before SAM

Motion-graphics layers mostly have hard edges and flat colour, so k-means colour regions, connected components, a median clean plate after stabilisation, and OCR boxes separate most elements for free. For props, **sam3.cpp** (MIT, ggml) measured these per-frame CPU tracking times at 1008 px on an Apple M4 Pro with 4 threads: **EdgeTAM 1.1 s, SAM 2.1 Tiny about 2.5 s, SAM 3 23.8 s** ([GitHub](https://github.com/PABannier/sam3.cpp)). That makes EdgeTAM about **25 minutes per object for a 45 s reel**. Run it at 512 px with stride 2-3, inside a single shot only, and seed it with one SAM 3 concept-prompt keyframe per shot. SAM 3 and 3.1 are GPU-class models: about 848M parameters, with 3.1 claiming about 7x speed-up at 128 objects on an H100 ([SAM 3, arXiv 2511.16719](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16719v2); [LearnOpenCV](https://learnopencv.com/sam-3-whats-new/)). Diffusion omnimattes (**Generative Omnimatte**, **EasyOmnimatte**) are GPU-only and shift colours, which is fatal for SSIM matching ([arXiv 2512.21865](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21865)). Keep them for the P4 experiment.

### Text and fonts: OCR finds the boxes, and rendering candidate fonts settles the font

**RapidOCR** (Apache-2.0 code and PP-OCRv5-derived models) returns per-word and per-character boxes when called with `return_word_box=True, return_single_char_box=True` ([GitHub](https://github.com/RapidAI/RapidOCR); [v3.2.0](https://newreleases.io/project/github/RapidAI/RapidOCR/release/v3.2.0)). These boxes come from the recognizer's CTC column positions inside a straight line box ([DeepWiki](https://deepwiki.com/RapidAI/RapidOCR/3.4-word-box-calculation)). They are approximate, and they fail on rotated text. A 300x400 image took about **242 ms end to end** on an unnamed CPU, so OCR on every frame of a 60 s reel would take tens of minutes. Instead, run OCR when pixels change and on the sharpest settled frame of each word. Track boxes between OCR frames, and read the reveal style (per-letter alpha, blur-in via Laplacian, pop or slide via box scale and offset) from pixels inside the known box. Correct OCR text against the VO transcript, which also gives "word appears when spoken" timing.

For fonts, **DINOv2-LoRA on GoogleFontsBench** reports **99.0% top-1 on v2**, up from 86% on v1. The benchmark covers only **394 variants of about 31 families** of synthetic crops ([arXiv 2602.13889](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13889v2); weights [`dchen0/font_classifier_v4`](https://huggingface.co/dchen0/font_classifier_v4)). One report says the v4 ONNX export may run at an untrained input resolution ([PR](https://github.com/Confect-io/google-font-classifier/pull/1)). **storia font-classify** (EfficientNet-B3, ONNX) covers about 3,000 Google Fonts ([HF](https://huggingface.co/storia/font-classify-onnx)). VLMs are poor at recognising fonts ([arXiv 2503.23768](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.23768v2)). Use both classifiers to produce a shortlist. Then render each candidate at the measured size and score IoU or chamfer distance against the binarised glyph mask, the method `stroke-reveal` used to rebuild a typeface reveal frame-accurately ([GitHub](https://github.com/Sama-ndari/stroke-reveal)). This also recovers weight, size, tracking and leading. Paid AE display fonts will be outside both label sets, so the realistic goal is the nearest free font, confirmed with the user.

### Eases: segment the curve, fit a Bézier, snap to a named ease

This stage closes the "feel" gap and needs no ML. Segment each tracked channel (x, y, scale, rotation, opacity) into holds and moves with **ruptures** (BSD-2, Pelt or Dynp) or Ramer-Douglas-Peucker ([GitHub](https://github.com/deepcharles/ruptures)). Normalise each segment to the unit square and fit `cubic-bezier(x1,y1,x2,y2)`. Once t is solved per sample by Newton iteration, y1 and y2 are linear in the least-squares problem ([MATLAB LSQ](https://fr.mathworks.com/matlabcentral/fileexchange/15542-cubic-bezier-least-square-fitting)). Snap to the nearest library ease when its error is close to the free fit's. Standard Penner eases are well approximated by Béziers; easeOutCubic matches `(0.33, 1, 0.67, 1)` **within 0.5%** ([vawe.dev](https://vawe.dev/easing/ease-out-cubic)). Detect elastic and bounce separately, from velocity sign changes. Fit spatial paths separately from the speed graph. Our own estimate, unverified: at 30 fps a 10-frame move has enough samples to separate ease-in, ease-out and linear, but not cubic from quart. Only the synthetic benchmark can measure the real confusion rates.

### Audio: separate VO from music, then take timing from each stem

**faster-whisper** (MIT) with `word_timestamps=True` transcribed 13 minutes of audio in **1m42s with `small` int8** on an 8-thread i7 ([GitHub](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper)). By extrapolation, a 60 s reel takes about 15-20 s on 4 cores. Add **WhisperX** (BSD-2) wav2vec2 alignment for tighter word edges. It gives no timing for numbers or currency ([GitHub](https://github.com/m-bain/whisperX)). For stems, **audio-separator** (MIT) wraps BS-RoFormer (**vocal SDR 12.9**) and htdemucs_ft (**10.8**) ([GitHub](https://github.com/nomadkaraoke/python-audio-separator)). RoFormers are slow on CPU, so htdemucs, run once and cached, is the practical default. Use **beat_this** (MIT, code and weights) for beats and downbeats, without madmom's NC DBN ([GitHub](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this)). Use **PANNs** (MIT) or LAION-CLAP for whoosh, hit and riser tags on onsets in the residual ([PANNs](https://github.com/qiuqiangkong/audioset_tagging_cnn); [CLAP](https://github.com/LAION-AI/CLAP)). CLAP's checkpoints carry training-data caveats, so prefer PANNs for anything automated.

## Fit-by-render only works when the search is tiny

Our WebGL renderer takes 0.5-5 s per frame and is not differentiable, so search must be the last step, not the first. **CMA-ES** suits black-box inverse rendering of up to about 100 parameters ([FIDE, arXiv 2607.17411](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17411v1)). Nevergrad's documentation calls CMA excellent when "the budget is large, e.g. 1000 x the dimension" ([nevergrad](https://facebookresearch.github.io/nevergrad/optimization.html)). By our arithmetic, a 6-parameter fit at 100·D evaluations over 3 frames is about **30 minutes per segment** at 1 s per frame. That is far too slow for every keyframe. The workable plan:

1. Measure all parameters directly first.
2. Recognise the archetype, so only a handful of parameters are free.
3. Render once, and compute a masked loss per layer, so a joint problem over L layers becomes L independent problems with D ≈ 2-10 each.
4. Run warm-started CMA-ES (small sigma, 10-30·D evaluations), or GP/TPE Bayesian optimisation when D ≤ 10, only on parameters with large residuals.
5. Render candidates in parallel across 4 workers, coarse to fine from 180p to 360p.

Pin pycma seeds: nevergrad 1.0.12's CmaFmin2 runs unseeded and so is not reproducible ([arXiv 2611.06879](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06879v1)). A PyTorch affine-grid compositor that mirrors our layer model can provide cheap gradients for joint transform fits. Initialisation is what made sprite decomposition fast, not the optimiser ([arXiv 2408.03923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03923)). Use cheap losses (masked L1 at low resolution, mask IoU, centroid and box error) inside the loop. Keep SSIM and LPIPS for scoring, since assets differ and LPIPS cost on CPU is unmeasured.

## Every run should leave the system measurably better

The self-improvement loop rests on one fact: our engine is a perfect generator of labelled data. Document-layout work shows that procedural synthetic data trains strong detectors without hand labels: DocLayout-YOLO's DocSynth-300K ([GitHub](https://github.com/opendatalab/DocLayout-YOLO)) and RanLayNet ([arXiv 2404.09530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09530v2)). The Kinetic Typography Diffusion Model built about 600K training videos from 584 templates ([arXiv 2407.10476](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10476)). Applied to us, the loop has five parts. The rest of this section is our design.

**Run logs as data.** Every run writes `extract/runs/<reel>/`: inputs, the version of every stage, per-stage outputs, fit scores, timings and failures. That makes any later change replayable against past reels.

**User corrections become regression tests.** When the user fixes a cut frame, an ease, a font or an archetype label, store it as a labelled assertion with its tolerance (for example, cut at frame 212 ± 0). CI then re-runs the extractor on that reel. A change that breaks an approved correction fails the build.

**A frozen synthetic benchmark.** Sample template, layer count, assets, keyframe times, ease class (named and free Béziers), transition type and camera parameters. Render at 360p and 30 fps through the real engine. Then degrade the clips the way downloads are degraded: H.264 at varied CRF, rescaling, 24/25/60-to-30 fps conversion, VFR, blur. Score cut error in frames, keyframe-time error, ease-class confusion, camera RMSE, font top-k and per-shot SSIM. Combined with the r1 ground truth (the real AE project), this is the gate every change must pass without regressing. For ease classifiers, generate trajectories analytically with simulated tracking noise rather than rendering pixels, which yields millions of examples in seconds. Use renders only to calibrate the noise model.

**Scheduled retraining.** Small CPU models (LightGBM or a 1D-CNN for ease class; a logistic or MLP head on frozen embeddings for transition and archetype) retrain on synthetic data plus every approved real label. Optionally fine-tune TransNetV2 on rendered transitions on a briefly rented GPU. Ship a new model only if it beats the old one on both the synthetic and the real regression sets.

**Priors and `LEARNINGS.md`.** Approved shots join the archetype library with their measured eases, durations and transition statistics. These become priors that narrow the next fit, and threshold changes are written to `LEARNINGS.md`. Per the standing rules, only user-approved shots enter the library.

## Conclusion

Our real advantage over published research is the renderer, not access to better models. Academic systems must recover an unknown generative process. We already own a generator that draws this style exactly, so extraction becomes classification against a known vocabulary plus low-dimensional fitting, and every render is free ground truth. That reverses the usual priority: the synthetic benchmark and the ease fitter are worth more than any GPU model, and should be built before SAM, omnimattes or 3D solvers. A rented GPU earns its cost in only two places: concept-prompted prop masks on keyframes, and occasional fine-tuning of the cut and transition detector.

The biggest open risk is empirical, not architectural. No source measures OCR, font classification, flow or ease recovery on stylised motion graphics, and none of the CPU timings here were checked on our container. The first milestone should therefore be the measurement harness itself: decode r1, render a few hundred synthetic shots, and time and score each tier A component on them. Every ranking in this report should then be re-ordered by those numbers rather than by the literature.
